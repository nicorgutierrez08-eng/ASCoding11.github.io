- `e63334287a3deaa3/rumble-karts/`: Rumble Karts (Back Room), a 3D kart-battle game for up to 12 players (Three.js, Firebase Realtime Database rooms under `rooms/<code>` with no prefix, so any invite code without a two-letter game prefix opens it; same project and rules as the other Back Room games). There is no Quick match: the menu's room code (a random word-number by default, or an invite's) is the room. Rooms hold up to 12 karts (`MAX_ROOM`): a full code says "Room is full"; the plain code `lobby` spreads to `lobby-2` … `lobby-6`. Modes: Classic (most wrecks in 3 minutes), Gun Game (level up through all 14 weapons; the ladder is `GG_LADDER`) and Zone. Each player writes only their own node `rooms/<code>/players/<uid>` (`n, c, x, z, h, s, st, hp, sh, tb, sp, gv, fz, gh, tn, d, sc, sm, sr, ts`), 20 times a second while anything changes; other karts are drawn from a short history of those updates (`applySnapshot`/`updateRemote`, the same idea as `netsmooth.js`). There is no host: the round record `rooms/<code>/round` (`{id, ends, st, m, w}`) is written with transactions by whoever notices it has run out, and the round number picks the map, the special drops and the zone spots. Everything else goes through `rooms/<code>/events/<push>` (removed after 5 s): shots, throws and hits. The shooter's computer decides what a rocket, blaster, boomerang, buffalo or tornado hit and sends a `hit` event, but only the victim's own computer applies damage to its kart and reports the wreck with a `kill` event (which is what credits the attacker), so no computer can hurt a kart it doesn't own. Practice vs bots runs the same code offline with `Net.send` looping events straight back. Items come out of ? boxes by the weights in `ITEMS` (`w`, relative odds; `tier` sets the frame colour and tier 2–3 pickups are announced to the room); epic items drop on the map every 30 s and Arjun's Gandiva with 30 s left. Two named weapons: Nico's Cream Cannon (common, two shots per pickup: a blob of whipped cream lobbed in a short arc, projectile kind `cream`; a hit event carries `cr`, the seconds of slow-down; every computer paints goo on the kart named and the victim's own computer takes 15 damage, drops to 55% top speed for 3 s and splatters its screen for 2.5 s; the goo washes off over about four seconds) and The Angry Whip (legendary, weight 1, half as common as the Gandiva; one use). The whip is the only weapon with a two-step hit: `wh` is the wind-up everyone sees (0.4 s telegraph, the target glows red and gets a note; the target's computer counts the 0.4 s from when the wind-up reaches it, so lag can't shorten the warning), then the target's own computer decides whether the lasso caught it (alive, no spawn shield or Spikes shield, not a ghost, not already caught, still within reach of a living attacker) and answers `whh` (caught: every computer lifts that kart from the event's time stamp for 1.2 s with three cracks, then slams it down, and the target's computer wrecks its own kart with weapon `whip`, so the attacker gets the wreck like any other; if another computer hears of the wreck before its own lift has finished, it slams the kart down there and then) or `whm` (missed: the whip cracks at empty air). Bots use both with the same rules and will Warp or Ghost out of a wind-up if they happen to hold one. Kill-feed text for the two reads "got creamed by" and "got whipped by". Nothing is stored between sessions except the driver name, paint, last room, mode and sound setting in the browser; the Leaderboards get play time, wrecks and wins through `stats.js`. On touch screens it shows a stick and a Fire button; `window.__rumble` exposes the game's objects for debugging and tests.
- `e63334287a3deaa3/shell-crackers/`: Shell Crackers (Back Room), a first-person egg shooter (Three.js, Firebase Realtime Database rooms under `rooms/sc-…`, same project and rules as Rumble Karts). Maps are block worlds (72 × 72 m; blocks are 1 m wide and 0.5 m tall, so stairs are rows of half-metre steps) built by the functions in `MAPS` (`buildFarm`, `buildFactory`, `buildTown`) from the tools above them: `fill`/`clear`, `building` (walls, floors, windows, doors, flat or gabled roofs), `stairs`, `stairsDown`, `tunnel`, and small props. The ground is 3 m thick so tunnels fit under it, and every call is also placed turned 180° about the centre so both halves match. Bots path over a navigation graph of every spot an egg can stand. The Training range (menu button under Practice) is its own map, `buildRange` with `RANGE_MAP`, kept out of `MAPS` so it never comes up in practice or online rotation: bullseye boards at 10/20/30/45/55 m, balloons that pop and come back, and unarmed dummies (`DUMMY_ROUTES`) that strafe or patrol and respawn after being cracked. Every gun is on keys 1–5 (6 is the pistol) and T resets the stats; the number keys stay fixed there, while T is the rebindable "Reset training stats" action in the Controls panel (`keybinds.js`) and the Pistol and Swap keys work on the range too. Magazines still need reloading but never run out, yolk bombs refill, and it has no clock, no rating and no database writes; its hit, accuracy and headshot counts live only in the open tab (`Range` in the page). On touch screens it adds a button for each gun and a Reset button (`#tTrain`) to the usual touch controls. Ranked rating: Elo from each match's finishing order, multiplied by 1.25^(real opponents − 1) (×1 for one real opponent, capped at ×3; bots-only practice ×0.4; first 10 matches move faster; leaving an online match after 45 s counts as last place). Ratings are stored at `rooms/sc-ratings/players/<uid>`, which the existing rules allow; each player's own browser writes their rating, so it isn't cheat-proof. Online, each egg writes its own `players/<uid>` node about 12 times a second while anything changed (position, aim, health, shots), twice more right after it stops changing (`Net.wantSend`, so others see it stop at once instead of carrying it on into the wall or floor it stopped at) and once a second otherwise; other eggs are dropped after 9 s without an update. The match lives in `rooms/sc-<code>/round` (set by a transaction on joining, replaced when it is 20 s past its end); the last egg to leave the room removes it (`Net.dropRound`, only when no other egg is in the room and only the match it played), so an unused code doesn't keep a stale node, and `rematch` starts a fresh match if there is none. The round's `map` decides the map: joining waits up to 8 s for the round before dropping you in, and another egg is only drawn, hittable or heard while its `players` node carries the same match id (`m`) as yours, so eggs on different rounds never see or hit each other. When a party lands together, every browser races to start the round with its own random map; the join and rematch transactions run with `applyLocally` off so nobody acts on a losing guess, and a round with the same id but a different map from the one built still switches maps (`Net.isNewRound`). Frame cost: hills and trees are merged into four meshes (one per quarter round the map, so the ones behind you aren't drawn), each cloud into one, each egg's face into two and each gun into one geometry shared by every egg and the view model (`mergeParts`, colours as vertex colours), and particles and shell shards are instanced pools (`instPool`), so a busy practice match is about 60 draw calls instead of about 220. A slow frame is played in steps of at most 1/30 s (`simulate`; only frames longer than `FRAME_CAP`, 0.25 s, lose time), so a slow laptop no longer plays in slow motion below 20 frames a second. If frames stay under about 30 a second for 8 s of play even at `autores.js`'s lowest resolution, and the player has never picked a graphics setting, the game switches itself to Fast (no shadows), says so in the feed and saves that; Pretty in Settings puts it back. Collision: an egg is a box 0.64 m wide and 1.45 m tall (`PR`, `PH`) against the blocks. Movement goes one axis at a time in steps under 0.2 m, steps up onto ledges of up to `STEP` (0.6 m) with head room, and falls are capped at 30 m/s (`MAX_FALL`), so normal movement never ends inside a block at any frame rate. If something does put an egg inside one, `pushOut` moves it out the shortest way that ends clear, passes through no other block and doesn't take the egg's middle through a wall: sideways, down from under a ceiling, or up only onto a top within a step. (The old code lifted it half a metre at a time until it was clear, which could launch it up to 12 m onto a roof or out of a building.) If there is no such way within 1.5 m, `rescue` puts it on the nearest spot an egg can stand. The webdriver-only test hook (`window.__sc`) exposes the movement code for the collision audit. Spawns: each map builder places them with `spawn(x,z)` (mirrored like everything else), and each must be a spot an egg fits (`standV`: something solid underneath and 1.5 m of air above; the egg's box fits inside that block column). A spawn that a map edit has blocked is moved to the nearest clear spot on the same level within 3 m, with a console warning (`spawnSpot`), instead of being lifted onto whatever covers it, and `respawn` never hatches an egg inside a block (it shoves it clear or uses the nearest standing spot). All current spawns are clear; after changing a map, check that the console shows no `spawn moved` or `spawn blocked` warning. Other eggs are drawn with `netsmooth.js`, carried on for at most 200 ms (`REMOTE_AHEAD`) when an update is late, and `drawClamp` keeps them in the map and out of blocks (pushed out the same way as above, and when carried on, no more than half a metre below or a quarter metre above where they were heading), so nobody is drawn, or shot at, inside a wall or a floor.

- `e63334287a3deaa3/netsmooth.js`: smooth movement for other players in the online games. Updates over school Wi-Fi and Firebase arrive late and in bursts, so a game that moves someone to their newest update makes them lurch and stall, and one that shows them a fixed time in the past freezes them whenever an update is later than that. Each remote player gets a `NetSmooth.lag()`, which measures how late their updates really arrive and shows them just far enough in the past that the next one is almost always already here (about a tenth of a second on good Wi-Fi, more on bad, never more than 0.45 s), easing that delay so the timeline never jumps; `NetSmooth.sample()` draws them between the two updates around that moment and carries them on for up to 0.3 s if an update is late. Used by Shell Crackers, Backrooms 1v1–4v4, Hail Mary, Coil, Quarry, Chromaflage, Odd One Out, Spire, Turbo Trail, Escapade and Updraft. Kartwheel, Slipstream, Boostball, Rumble Karts and Tumble Trials keep their own approach: cars, karts and racers are projected forward to the present from their speed, which matters more there than perfectly even motion. Settings were tuned with a simulation of good, school and bad Wi-Fi: compared with chasing the newest update, other players stall about a fifth as often and almost never lurch, for roughly a tenth of a second more delay.
