Joukowsky Dynamics

A road car whose four wheels become ducted proprotors. Each wheel is a tyre on a duct rim with a 7-blade rotor inside; to fly, the car stands on landing legs and each wheel swings 90° up and outward on a hinge. Every part is modelled as a CAD solid, and the rotor blades are designed with the equations from NASA's Fundamental Proprotor Design Considerations.

  • CadQuery / OpenCascade
  • Three.js
  • Rotor aerodynamics
  • Momentum theory
  • Holographic sequence: watch the car assemble, drive, stand on its legs, open its petals, swing its wheels up into rotors and take off
  • Click any region (a wheel, the battery, the cockpit) to fly in and see it pulled apart, with a live briefing of what it's doing at that moment
  • 242 unique parts and 3,607 placed pieces, each with a datasheet, a three-view drawing and its own STEP file
  • Bill of materials, mass roll-up and centre of gravity computed from the CAD
  • Rotor calculator: blade twist and chord, stall margins, edgewise flow, power vs airspeed and hover endurance
  • Honest numbers: the analysis shows where the design falls short and what would fix it

Tideline

A radar satellite built to watch ports and shipping lanes. It uses synthetic-aperture radar (SAR) at X-band, so it sees ships through cloud, rain and darkness. A 4.1 m folding phased array with 680 transmit / receive modules images a berth at under 1 m or a 120 km stretch of shipping lane. On board it finds the ships, checks them against their AIS position reports to flag dark vessels, and sends alerts about blocked channels or anchorage queues within minutes.

  • CadQuery / OpenCascade
  • Three.js
  • Radar engineering
  • Orbital mechanics
  • Holographic mission: separation, solar wing and radar wings unfolding, a roll to point the beam, imaging a port, and the ship list going down
  • 44 unique parts and 1,547 placed pieces, every module, hinge and battery cell modelled, each with a datasheet and STEP file
  • Radar and mission physics calculated live: noise floor, ambiguities, smallest detectable ship, revisit, power, data, slewing and propellant, with 27 pass / fail checks
  • Port analytics: a simulated SAR image of a port where a real detector finds ships, matches AIS and spots congestion and blockages
  • A tabletop prototype you can build for about $400: a 60 GHz radar on a 1 m rail that images a model harbour through a cardboard “cloud”

Harvest Steps

A floor mat that turns footsteps at the Union Square Greenmarket into electricity. Each step pushes a 60 cm tile down 6 mm; racks and one-way clutches spin four small generators, and the energy flows mat to mat on a 24 V line to a battery cart that charges vendors' phones and card readers and lights stalls after dark. Every step is counted too, giving the market a real footfall number.

  • Three.js
  • Electromagnetic harvesting
  • Power electronics
  • Arduino
  • Step sequence: follow one footstep from heel strike through the gears and generators to the battery, with live force, speed and energy readouts
  • Exploded blueprint of every part, each with its material, size, estimated mass and job
  • Energy physics you can change: walker weight, plate travel, efficiencies and crowd size give joules per step, kWh per market day and pass / fail checks
  • A schematic market day where shoppers light up as they cross the mats, for three levels of coverage
  • A full build guide for a bench prototype (about $127): schematic, a click-through soldering walkthrough on a perfboard layout, multimeter checks, and an Arduino sketch that measures joules per step
  • Honest numbers: about 2 kWh on a busy day, so sponsors and footfall data have to pay for it, not the electricity

Applied Robotics Workbench

The electronics for a small two-wheel rover, drawn as six circuit sheets: the power tree, a MOSFET switch, a dual motor driver, and three sensors (ultrasonic range, line following and an I²C bus). Every sheet says why each part is there and which numbers to check on the bench with a multimeter.

  • Circuit design
  • Arduino / ESP32
  • Power electronics
  • SVG schematics
  • Six schematics with component values, a supply / signal / ground colour code and a title block on every sheet
  • Level shifting done properly: a 5 V ultrasonic sensor read safely by a 3.3 V ESP32
  • Bench calculators for voltage dividers, LED resistors (rounded to standard values) and battery runtime
  • An Arduino sketch that drives both motors with the pins exactly as drawn, plus a parts list and a safe power-up order

Works on phones and computers. A reference design for learning; check each part's datasheet before building.

Project Daedalus

A compact Bluetooth game controller with one magnetic stick that cannot wear into drift, four face buttons and four shoulder buttons, designed to be made in a batch of 30: a circuit board assembled by a factory, a printed case, and firmware that makes it a standard gamepad on Windows and Mac.

  • ESP32-C6
  • Bluetooth LE HID
  • Circuit board design
  • 3D printing
  • The whole plan on one page: why each part was chosen, a priced parts list, and every pin assignment
  • Arduino firmware you can download: a press is reported on first contact, the stick calibrates itself, and it asks the computer for the fastest link it allows
  • Board and case guidance written for a first-time builder, with step-by-step assembly
  • A printable case you can download and turn in 3D, drawn from the same numbers as the circuit board
  • Honest numbers: $22 to $27 a unit at 30, not the $10 to $15 hoped for, and what it would really cost to sell a Bluetooth product
